— Overview · Tool introduction —

What Blocking Board solves

A digital rehearsal tool that checks whether actors, space, and cameras collide before generating video.

1
PLAN / Floor plan
Organize positions, routes, and props.
2
PREVIEW / 3D
Play the scene to check height, eyelines, and collisions.
3
OUTPUT / Cuts
Carry the result into the timeline and prompts.
— Note 01 · Core concept —

Before generating a video, Blocking Board makes it possible to check whether the actors, space, and cameras collide — a digital rehearsal tool.

— Note 02 · Order —

Set positions and routes on the floor plan, review height and eyelines on the 3D stage, and carry the result into cuts, the timeline, and prompts.

“Review and adjust visually before you shoot”

What matters more than auto-generation itself is creating an intermediate state that can be reviewed.

Script→Floor plan→3D stage→Cuts / Output

— Workflow · 5 steps —

Five steps from start to finish

Create and review a scene in this order. You can go back to an earlier step at any time to revise.

1
SCENE
Script and prompt input
2
SPACE
Floor plan and prop placement
3
PATH
Actor and route placement
4
PREVIEW
Preview and review in 3D
5
EXPORT
Prompt · Video · Shotbook
— Step 01 · Scene —

Enter a script and prompt to create a draft. Refine the space and props, then set each actor’s starting point and route.

— Step 02 · Review —

Play the scene on the 3D stage to check for collisions and eyeline problems. If you find a problem, go back to the staging step and fix it..

— Step 03 · Export —

When the review is done, export the cuts, timeline, video prompt, and shotbook.

Structure the script and generate a draft with 1-Click

Turn natural language into stage data. The clearer the fields, the more stable the result.

— Staging · Space / Actors / Routes —

Place the space, then place the actors and routes

Auto-placement is only a starting point. Refine the space, place the actors, then draw routes on the floor plan.

— Step 01 · Space —

From the left panel, Add Objectselect it and pick a category to place walls, doors, windows, and furniture on the floor plan.

— Step 02 · Actors —

Place the actors and specify their names, body types, starting positions, and directions.

— Step 03 · Routes —

Click waypoints on the floor plan to draw a route. Enter to confirm, Esc to cancel.

— Step 04 · Fine adjustments —

In the 3D tools, E to move · R to rotate · T to resize. One floor-plan grid cell = 1 m — Use this as the scale reference. —

Chapter 07Cameras

— Cameras · Multicam —

Design the cameras and shot sizes

Define each camera’s role, and set its shot size, angle of view, tracking target, and movement.

— Note 01 · Camera management —

From the camera panel, CAM1~4manage them. For each camera, set the shot size, angle of view (FOV), tracking target, and movement.

— Note 02 · Shot sizes —

WIDE frames the actor’s full body, MEDIUM handles dialogue, CLOSE catches reactions, OTS is used for over-the-shoulder views.

— Note 03 · Panel settings —

Check the lens and FOV, tracking target, camera movement, angle, and height Z (m).

— Tip —

Before worrying about lens numbers, first decide what that shot needs to show..

Chapter 08Cuts and timing
P. 008
08
— Time · Cuts and dialogue —

Place cuts and dialogue on the timeline

A cut is the unit of story time. Place them so actor movements and dialogue do not overlap awkwardly.

— Step 01 · Cuts —

Add cuts to the master timeline and set their lengths and cameras. Check for gaps between cuts and continuity warnings.

— Step 02 · Dialogue —

Dialogue is managed on a separate track. Check who speaks when, and align the dialogue with the action segments.

— Step 03 · Playback —

Use the bottom timeline’s play, pause, and scrub. Check poses at specific frames and review collisions across the full movement.

— Note · FPS and curves —

FPS adjusts the speed of the final video, and curves adjust changes in movement speed.

Chapter 09Direction check

— Check · Direction QA —

Review the direction check before playback

Check the results and fix eyeline, 180-degree axis, collision, and route problems.

✓
Pass
No problems under the current conditions
✕
Issue found
Eyeline, axis, or collision problems must be fixed
·
Info
An item shown for reference without a verdict
— Check 01 · Eyeline —

Check whether each camera’s view cone points at its target actor.

— Check 02 · Axis —

Mind the action line between actor and camera, the so-called 180-degree axis..

— Check 03 · Collision —

Check whether actors overlap or pass through walls, and whether any route is unreachable.

— Fix —

If the direction check shows a warning, go back to staging or route setup, fix it, and play again.

— Advanced · Photo Path —

Previz and advanced tools

Align to a real location with Photo Path, and compare alternative direction plans side by side.

— Step 01 · Previz —

Play the scene and export a previz video (WebM). Use it to align timing and framing with the team before final generation.

— Step 02 · Photo Path —

Load a photo of the real location, align 4 floor reference points, then mark actor positions and routes.

— Step 03 · Direction plan A/B —

Saving a direction plan preserves the original even if you change the camera or routes. Before trying something new, name the original plan and note its purpose.

— Note —

A previz video is not a final deliverable , but an intermediate result for agreeing on blocking and cuts.

— Summary · Wrap-up —

Good blocking gives the next person in line confidence

A summary of the process for reviewing a scene, from script to export.

Order of a scene

Script → Space → Actors → Cameras → Timing → Check → Export. If a problem comes up, go back to an earlier step and fix it.

When entering a scene

Write events and actions as time segments, and use consistent names for actors, props, and cameras.

When reviewing

Plan routes on the floor plan and play the scene in 3D. Resolve eyeline, collision, and continuity warnings.

When handing off

After review, export the English prompt, shot list, PNG, and shotbook PDF. Compose the final input in the order “Scene / Title / Actors / Props / Cameras / Time · Action.”
