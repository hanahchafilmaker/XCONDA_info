1. What is the XCONDA website?

XCONDA is an AI video and image production platform operated by CUBEBERRY. It brings multiple generative AI models together and provides a connected set of production tools, from a script to a storyboard and then to video generation. Its central principle is simple.

Direct first, then generate — plan the direction before generating the content.

Why start with direction? Video AI redraws each cut from scratch. It does not remember the previous cut. Unless you establish the space, characters, and composition first, the people and locations can change from cut to cut. XCONDA addresses this problem at the directing stage: establish the space with Turn, the characters and composition with FlexBoard, and the movement paths with Blocking Board before you generate.

XCONDA is developed and operated by CUBEBERRY itself. CUBEBERRY uses these tools to produce work broadcast on terrestrial television and KOCCA Content Lab AI web dramas. This manual follows the same order used in actual production.

The website uses a credit-based system. Each operation deducts a specified number of credits, and you can purchase credit packages to top up your balance. Supported languages are English, Korean, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese.

3. Asset Library — save what you create and reuse it

Assets created in XCONDA are stored in the library and reused in later work. Sets created in Turn are saved there as well. Character and background references, key images, storyboard projects, and generated images and videos are all assets. As your library grows, future work becomes faster and more consistent.

3-1. Character sheets — facial references for each character

Prepare a facial image as a character sheet for each character. This sheet is the reference that keeps the same character recognizable across cuts. Include character names in the script so the AI can connect the appropriate face and costume references. For detailed rules on creating character sheets, see the Prompt Writing Guide.

3-2. Background sheets — references for locations and spaces

Prepare a background image as a background sheet for each location. This includes four-sided background sheets created with Turn: front, left, right, and rear. The background sheet is the reference that keeps the same space consistent across cuts.

3-3. Turn sets — 360° virtual sets

Save the 360° virtual sets created in Turn to the library and reuse them in future work. There is no need to recreate the same space.

3-4. Key images — reference images for cuts

Key images extracted in Turn and cut images finalized in FlexBoard become the references for the next stage: video generation. An empty-space image (Freeze Frame) serves as a background reference for video generation, while a key image containing characters is used as a storyboard cut.

3-5. Projects — storyboard and scene projects

FlexBoard storyboard projects and Blocking Board scene projects are also assets. Open a project to revise cuts, generate again, and export the results.

The most important principle: until the characters and locations are established, you cannot create the references needed for the next stage. The Asset Library is the repository that supports this principle. Before starting new work, first check whether the library contains the required character and background sheets.

4. Creating images — the detailed FlexBoard workflow

Create images with FlexBoard. Break the script into instructions for individual cuts, then generate an image for each cut to check its composition and characters in advance. You can create storyboards of 6 to 36 cuts and generate images with GPT Image 2. The key is to revise only the cuts that do not meet your needs.

4-1. Step 01 · Enter — select Flex Board and create a new project

Select Flex Board in the Storyboard category. In the workspace, use New Project to create a project and open the project selection panel. You can also start with a sample to learn the workflow.

4-2. Step 02 · References — establish character and background references

Choose the character and background reference images. These references keep the characters and space consistent across cuts. Without references, the characters may change from cut to cut.

4-3. Step 03 · Text cuts — write each cut and preview the text

Divide the script into cuts and write instructions for each one. In the text preview, first check the cut cards, descriptions, and sound effects. Finalizing the cut order and composition at the text stage saves credits. The text preview is free and serves as the checkpoint before generation.

4-4. Step 04 · Image generation — compare each image in the grid

After generating images, compare each image in the grid with its description. Check whether the composition, characters, and background are correct for each cut.

4-5. Step 05 · Revise individual cuts — regenerate only what does not work

Select only the cuts you are unhappy with, revise their prompts, and generate again. Recreating a cut starts at 20 credits. To keep the same scene while changing only the angle or camera movement, drag an image to replace the intended cut.

4-6. Step 06 · Finalize — check the final cuts and camera settings

Review the final cuts and check that the camera settings—shot duration, shot size, camera angle, and camera movement—match your intention. Then move on to video generation.

Check in this order before sending to video: cut-grid images → scene descriptions → character tokens → whether any cuts are missing. Complete all four checks before sending.

6. Turn — create a 360° virtual set from one photo

Turn creates a 360° virtual set from one photograph or a text description. Because it builds the space first and then lets you move the camera inside it to choose an angle, doors, windows, and furniture remain in the same positions when the cut changes. Turn is the solution to background consistency.

6-1. Step 01 · Analyze the space — generate a 360° space from one photo

Provide one interior photograph or describe the space in text to generate a 360° environment.

6-2. Step 02 · 3D preview — rotate to find an angle and adjust dimensions

Look around the space in the 3D preview to find the angle you want. You can adjust the room size and ceiling height immediately; resizing does not require additional generation.

6-3. Step 03 · Edit walls — add props and replace wallpaper

Add props and change the wallpaper on each wall. Use the mask tool to specify the area to change, then enter a prompt to create a new prop. Leave the prompt empty to remove the selected content automatically.

6-4. Step 04 · Key images — establish characters, costumes, and cinematic style for 2K images

Add characters, costumes, and a cinematic style to the established frame and extract 1K/2K key images. These key images become references for storyboard cuts or video generation.

6-5. Use case A · Background sheets — create a four-sided background sheet

Put one side of a background sheet—the front image—into Turn, generate the left, right, and rear views, and group the four views into one set. This set becomes the background reference for video generation.

6-6. Use case B · Cut images — create cuts directly inside the set

Create a 360° virtual set, place characters and props inside it, then rotate to the desired angle and generate a cut image directly. Use that image as a storyboard cut.

Save sets created in Turn to the library and reuse them in later work. There is no need to recreate the same space.

7-2. Step 02 · Input — a prompt or start/end frames

Generate from text alone, or provide start/end frames, such as FlexBoard images, to establish the scene. Using images finalized in the storyboard helps preserve the characters and space.

7-3. Step 03 · Options — aspect ratio, resolution, duration, and high quality

Set the aspect ratio, resolution, duration, and high-quality option. Enabling the high-quality option improves quality.

7-4. Step 04 · Generate and review — check each cut and return to the storyboard if needed

Review the generated video cut by cut. If the characters or space have changed, return to the storyboard stage, establish the references, and generate again. Directing before generating is how you preserve consistency.

9. Blocking Board — rehearse movement before generating video

Blocking Board is a digital rehearsal tool for checking whether characters, the space, and the camera will collide before video generation. It is available as a free beta on the website, with no card required. Paste in a script to create a draft of the actors, space, movement paths, and cameras.

9-1. Step 01 · 1-Click — paste a script to create a draft

Paste in a script to generate a draft of the actors, space, movement paths, and cameras.

9-2. Step 02 · 2D floor plan — adjust placement and movement paths

Adjust actor and camera positions and their movement paths in the 2D floor plan.

9-3. Step 03 · 3D preview — play back the scene on a 3D stage

Play the scene on the 3D stage and review character and camera movement.

9-4. Step 04 · Direction checks — inspect collisions and eyelines

Check for collisions and eyeline issues in the direction review. If a problem appears, return to the floor plan and correct the placement or movement paths.

9-5. Step 05 · Export — export a shot book PDF and other deliverables

Reviewed scenes can be exported as English prompts for Seedance, a shot list, floor plans and frame PNGs, and a shot book PDF. You can also play back the scene and export a previsualization video in WebM format.

For complex scenes in which multiple characters cross paths, it is recommended that you establish positions and movement routes in Blocking Board first. If you generate without rehearsing, you will have to correct collisions and movement errors later. For detailed instructions, see the separate 13-chapter Blocking Board guide.

10. Final checks — a checklist before generation

The key to using the website is the order of operations. Direct first, then generate. Establish the composition and characters in the storyboard, review movement in Blocking Board, and then generate with Video Generator to reduce the number of regenerations.

  • Are the character sheets and background sheets in the library?
  • Have the cut order and instructions for each cut been finalized?
  • Have you compared the cut-grid images, scene descriptions, and character tokens?
  • Are any cuts missing?
  • Do the shot duration, shot size, camera angle, and camera movement match your intention?
  • If a Turn set is needed, have you reused one from the library?
  • Have you checked the final cuts and camera settings before sending to video?
