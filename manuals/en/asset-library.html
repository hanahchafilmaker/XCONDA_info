— Overview · Two Shelves, One Idea —

What the Asset Library Holds

The library holds two kinds of material: what you upload yourself, and what your tools produce. Those two tabs are the whole library, and every generator picks from them.

1
UPLOADS
Files you add by hand: faces, costumes, props, backgrounds, style frames.
2
GENERATIONS
Everything the tools produced, stacked by date, with model and ratio in the label.
3
PICK
Chosen inside a generator, on the Asset Library tab of the result panel.
4
REUSE
Confirmed with Use selected assets, then pointed at with @ in the prompt.
— Note 01 · The Library Generates Nothing —

It is the shelf you reach for before you generate: an approved face, a costume that must not drift, the wide shot of a set you will return to for weeks.

— Note 02 · Written Against the Live App —

Every screen here is the product as it stood in early September 2026, captured from a real account with real work in it. Nothing is a mock-up.

— Note 03 · What Is Not Here Yet —

No folders, tags, collections, favourites, versions, or usage lists appear in this build. Chapter 10 lists the gaps and the workarounds that hold until they ship.

— Note 04 · The Payoff —

You stop re-uploading the same face for every cut. A short approved set, picked in two clicks, replaces a folder hunt that used to eat the afternoon.

Screens Move —
Buttons get renamed between builds. Follow the flow, and confirm the label in your own window before you teach it to someone else.

— Entry · Getting In —

Opening the Library

There are two ways in: the library icon on the left rail, or Library in the account menu at the top right. That menu also carries credits, pricing, language, and admin.

— Step 01 · Open the Account Menu —

Click the avatar at the top right. The panel shows the account name and email, then a Credits gauge — here 0 of 2,824 — for whatever you spend next.

— Step 02 · Choose Library —

Library sits in that menu and stays highlighted while you are on the page, so the highlighted row itself tells you where you are.

— Step 03 · Know the Neighbours —

Pricing, QNA, Language, Admin, and Log out share the menu. Language switches the interface labels; this guide keeps those labels in English on purpose.

— Step 04 · Read the Credit Badge —

The number in the top bar is your remaining balance. Picking references costs nothing; the Generate button is where credits actually leave the account.

— Step 05 · Do Not Trust the Rail Alone —

Left-rail icons are unlabelled in this build. Hover before you click; the account-menu route is the one that is always named.

Account-Wide, Not Per Project

The same grid appears inside every tool that can reference an asset, which is why a name you can read matters more than where you filed the file.

Not a Local Folder

Sign out and the list goes with the account, not the machine. Anything you would cry about losing belongs on your own disk as well.

— Screen · Tabs, Filter, Grid —

The Library Screen

The page has three layers: a tab bar, a media filter, and the card grid. Under each card there is only a name and two icons — that is the entire metadata surface.

A
TAB BAR
Uploads for your own files, Generations for tool output. The active tab is the filled pill.
B
FILTERS
All · Images · Video · Audio on the right. Which options appear depends on the tab you are on.
C
CARD GRID
Thumbnail, name, download and delete icons. The first cell of Uploads is + Add asset.
— Step 01 · Read One Card —

A thumbnail, a filename, a download arrow, a bin. There is no type badge and no tag row, so the filename is the only description a colleague will ever see.

— Step 02 · Filter by Media —

Uploads offers Images, Video, and Audio; Generations offers Images and Video. Audio cards render as a waveform with the file name underneath.

— Step 03 · Download Is the Only Door Out —

The arrow saves the original file. Today it is your only way to back an asset up or hand it to someone outside the account.

— Step 04 · Delete Is Immediate —

The bin removes a single card. There is no visible undo, so read the name before you click, and ask the team first.

There Is No Search Box —
No search field, sort control, or collection tree exists on this screen. Your naming convention is the search function until one ships, which is why Chapter 5 is not optional reading.

— Add · The Only Way In —

Adding an Asset

The single entry point is + Add asset in the first cell of Uploads. The New Asset dialog takes a name, a description, and one media file; Create drops the card at the front of the grid.

+ Add asset→New Asset→Upload media→Create
— Step 01 · Open the Dialog —

Click the dashed first cell. One dialog covers images and audio; the file you attach decides which kind of card the grid then shows.

— Step 02 · Name Within 100 Characters —

Enter asset name counts to 100; Add description counts to 500. Fill the name first — the counter is the one limit you will actually hit.

— Step 03 · Attach the Media —

Upload media accepts a drag and drop or a click. Attach the file before pressing Create, or you leave an empty card that nobody can use.

— Step 04 · Write the Description You Will Want Later —

The description is where conditions live: key light, wardrobe state, angle, and what must not change. Two characters of it, as in the next figure, buys you nothing in a month.

— Step 05 · Create or Cancel —

Create commits. The name you typed becomes the card label and the card lands at the front of Uploads — it is not sorted into anything.

Renaming Is Not Obvious —
This build shows no card edit path. Treat the dialog as the one chance to get the name right; re-upload rather than hunt for a rename button that may not exist.

— Naming · The Index You Have —

Names & Descriptions That Survive

With no tags and no folders, the name is the index and the description is the memory. Ten seconds spent on both keeps the library usable in week six.

— Step 01 · Front-Load the Kind of Thing —

Start with the category, then the variant: char_namyoung_side, prop_phone_black, bg_office_wide. The product does it already — Office_angle1_wide, audio_partA_25s.

— Step 02 · Stay Short Enough to Read in a Card —

Cards truncate. Two tokens usually survive; five do not. Put the distinguishing word first so the visible part is the useful part.

— Step 03 · Describe Reproduction Conditions —

In the description, write what someone needs to rebuild the frame: lens feel, key light, wardrobe state, and the one thing that must not move.

— Step 04 · Agree on One Spelling —

Short Korean names appear in the live library, and so do English snake_case files. Either works, as long as the team uses one form for one subject.

— Step 05 · Fake Categories with Prefixes —

Until collections exist, char_, bg_, prop_, and audio_ are your folder tree. They cost nothing and make a flat grid scannable.

Name Before You Import

The dialog is the index. A minute spent there replaces a search that will not exist for months.

The Description Earns Its 500 Characters

Write the conditions of the shot, not the mood. Conditions are what you will have forgotten by the time it matters.

One Subject, One Spelling

Two spellings of one character quietly split your library in half, and the wrong half gets picked under deadline.

— Generations · The Archive —

The Generations Tab

Generations is the archive of what your tools produced. Dates divide the page, and every label carries the model, the cut number, and the aspect ratio.

— Step 01 · Read the Date Headers —

November 2 sits above October 27. Newest work is at the top, so the page doubles as a session log for the last few days.

— Step 02 · Decode a Label —

Kling Image 01 · 16:9 is model, cut number, ratio. Turn · 16:9 is a tool name and a ratio. The label tells you what made it; it never tells you what it was made from.

— Step 03 · Filter to Images or Video —

Generations offers Images and Video only. Audio lives under Uploads, because audio arrives as a file rather than as a generation.

— Step 04 · Promote a Result to an Asset —

Download the frame you want, switch to Uploads, and add it through + Add asset. That two-step move is how a good accident becomes reusable material.

— Step 05 · Know What the Archive Forgets —

The result stays; the reference set that produced it does not. Re-creating that combination is manual today, and the regeneration work in Chapter 10 is what changes it.

The Gap You Will Feel First —
When a face finally lands on the fifth try, the four failures and the winning set are gone from the screen. Promote the winner the same day, or you will rebuild the search from memory.

— Reuse · Inside Image Generator —

Using Assets in Image Generator

The image generator opens the library from the result panel: Reference, Asset Library, Options. The middle tab is the shelf, and Use selected assets is the hand that reaches in.

A
INPUT ASSETS
7 / 15 stored · Sending: Reference 7, with Manage to change what is attached.
B
PICKER
Saved assets, an IMAGE count, a VIDEO count. Empty sections say No saved assets.
C
ACTION
0 selected while nothing is ticked; Use selected assets stays disabled until you pick.
— Step 01 · Check the Counters First —

The Input Assets row reads stored against limit, and the hint underneath gives the real cap: up to 10 images per model. Numbers differ by model, so read them instead of assuming.

— Step 02 · Open Asset Library —

It is the middle tab of the panel that floats over your results. Reference shows uploads made for this run; Asset Library shows everything saved to the account.

— Step 03 · Tick Several at Once —

Each click toggles a yellow check and the footer count climbs — four selected in the figure below. This is the multi-select the old upload flow never had.

— Step 04 · Confirm with Use Selected Assets —

The button stays dim until something is selected, then attaches the whole set to Input Assets. A yellow button that refuses to press is a reminder, not a bug.

— Step 05 · Point at References with @ —

The panel says it plainly: use @ to point at an active reference in the prompt. Naming the asset beats describing the face again, every single time.

Selection Is Free, Generation Is Not —
Ten references cost the same as two. The Generate button on the same screen reads 48 credits, so spend the budget on the two or three frames that actually carry the look.

— Reuse · Frame Mode —

Video Generator & Frame Mode

The video generator reads the same library but assigns it differently. In frame mode an asset is not a reference; it is the start or the end of the shot.

— Step 01 · Confirm the Mode —

Under Models, Mode reads First/last frame. Frame mode decides the shot by its two endpoints, so choose those images with more care than you would choose a reference.

— Step 02 · Watch the Numbered Badges —

Every card in Input Assets carries a small numbered badge showing which endpoint it was assigned to. A badge means committed, not merely attached.

— Step 03 · Read the Grey Note —

Frame mode sends the assigned Start and End images; reference selections are preserved but inactive. Your picks are remembered, and they will not influence this render.

— Step 04 · Respect the Stored Count —

48 / 50 stored is one clean-up short of a wall. Prune before you are blocked, not after the shot is due.

— Step 05 · Audio Has Slots Too —

The AUDIO count in the library appears as three empty slots in the video panel. Audio can be attached here even though it cannot be generated here.

In Frame Mode, Prose Beats Pins —
The long prompt in the figure locks the shot in sentences: static camera, one continuous shot, no cut, no text. Use references to say who is in frame, and prose to say what happens.

— Limits · Three Numbers —

Working with Many Assets

The library grows faster than the panel can show it. Three different numbers govern how much you can attach, and all three are printed on screen.

01
STORED
Assets saved to the account — 48 / 50 in the video panel. A hard ceiling, not a suggestion.
02
PER RUN
What Input Assets carries into one generation — 7 / 15 in the image panel.
03
PER MODEL
The reference cap of the model itself, such as up to 10 images. Read it under the row.
— Step 01 · Tell the Three Numbers Apart —

Stored, per run, and per model are different limits that produce different errors. Mistaking one for another is how a promising run dies at the Generate button.

— Step 02 · Keep the Grid Short —

Cards stack four to eight per row depending on panel width. Past about three rows the panel pushes the prompt and the Generate button out of sight.

— Step 03 · Re-Confirm Selection After a Break —

Sign out or switch tools and the selection may or may not survive; the behaviour has been inconsistent. Reopen the tab and read the count before spending credits.

— Step 04 · Delete Only What Nobody Points At —

The bin deletes per card, and no usage list exists on screen. Ask the team before removing anything that appears in a delivered cut.

A Weekly Minute

Clear the upload queue, name what you added, and download the set you would hate to lose.

A Monthly Sweep

Remove duplicates and rejected looks. With no archive function, deleting after a local backup is the only tidy option.

A Project Close

Leave only the final set in the library so the next production starts from a decision, not from leftovers.

— Gaps · What Is Not Built —

What the App Does Not Do Yet

A plan built on features that do not exist fails quietly. This chapter records what the current build cannot do, from the development review of 1 September 2026.

COLLECTIONS
Collections

Not available. Prefixes in the file name are the substitute.

TAGS
Tags

Not available. The file name is the only searchable text.

VERSIONS
Versions

Not available. A new file is a new card; nothing links the two.

USAGE
Usage

No list of which cuts reference an asset. Ask before deleting.

REGENERATE
Regeneration

No way to reload the reference set behind an old result. In development for Image and Video Generator only.

SYNC
Sync

Some accounts report assets becoming unusable after a deployment. Cause and frequency still under investigation.

— Step 01 · Regeneration Is Not Built Yet —

Loading the reference set behind a past result is a first-phase development item, scoped to Image Generator and Video Generator. Do not promise it for other tools yet.

— Step 02 · Classification Is Manual —

No collections, tags, versions, or usage lists. The naming rules in Chapter 5 are not style advice; they are standing in for a database that has not shipped.

— Step 03 · Deployment Carries a Reported Risk —

Assets have become unusable for some accounts after a release, inconsistently and without a log entry. Frequency is unknown, so assume it can happen to you.

— Step 04 · Insure the Irreplaceable —

Download approved sets to local storage, keep one naming convention, and archive the final set when a project ships. Three habits, no dependencies.

This Chapter Ages Fastest —
It reflects the app as reviewed on 1 September 2026. When collections, versions, or regeneration ship, rewrite this page first and leave the other eleven alone.

— Wrap-Up · Habits That Hold —

Tips, Mistakes & Summary

Six habits that keep the library useful, and the mistakes that quietly turn it into a second downloads folder.

— Tip 01 · Name Before You Import —

The dialog is the last moment when the name is cheap. char_namyoung_side today saves a hunt in November.

— Tip 02 · One Subject, One Card —

Re-uploading a slightly different version of the same face creates two faces to choose from, and under deadline someone will choose wrong.

— Tip 03 · Promote Winners the Same Day —

The generation that finally works is only reusable once it is an asset. Do it while you still remember why it worked.

— Tip 04 · Keep the Working Set Under Ten —

The cap is a design decision, not an obstacle. Four good references beat ten that argue with each other.

— Tip 05 · Download What You Cannot Rebuild —

References you generated, and anything a client approved, belong on your own disk as well as in the library.

— Tip 06 · Read the Counters, Not Your Memory —

Stored, selected, and credits are printed on screen. Those three numbers prevent most failed runs.

What the Library Is For

Collect once, name it well, and let every tool point at it by name instead of by re-upload.

The Minimum Discipline

A readable name, a real description, and one media file — every time, no exceptions.

The Check That Saves a Cut

Read the counters, keep the set short, and download anything you could not reproduce.
