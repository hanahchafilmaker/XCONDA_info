Why running video first feels expensive

Seedance 2.5 gives good results, and it costs credits every run. If the first video misses the intent, you have to run it again from the start: a character changed, the background jumped, the cut order feels off.

Checking as images costs far less than checking as video. Put the two side by side and the difference is clear.

ItemCheck as video firstCheck as storyboard (images) first
When a cut is wrongRegenerate the whole videoRegenerate that one image
Time to checkWatch the video to the end to find itLay the cuts out on one screen and see in seconds
How to fixChange the prompt and regenerate repeatedlyEdit one cut
CreditsGrow a lot with every repeatMinimal, one image at a time

So before running video you need a stage where you look at "are these the right cuts?" as images. It is the same reason a drama set lines up the storyboard before the camera rolls.

FLEXBOARD is a storyboard engine

FLEXBOARD is a storyboard engine that turns a scenario into cut-by-cut images. Because it shows text and image together for every cut, you can check framing and characters before making video and fix only the cuts you do not like. It is designed to work with Seedance 2.5.

Open Storyboard in the left sidebar of XCONDA and the Flex Board card appears - the one labelled "a storyboard tool tuned for Seedance 2.5". Click it and choose New Project.

It was built around the places where video production usually stalls, and it has three core features.

  • The same actor stays. Face Lock keeps the same person across cuts. Cast and backgrounds are registered as cards, and each card automatically gets a label such as @image1 or @image2.
  • Regenerate only the cut you want to change. No rebuilding everything: just that cut.
  • Choose the image model. Pick from GPT Image 2, Seedream 5.0 Pro, GPT Image 2.5 Flare or GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst. If you are new, start with the default, GPT Image 2.

FLEXBOARD on screen

Here are the key screens in the order you meet them. Step-by-step instructions are in FLEXBOARD storyboards in 7 steps.

1) Enter the project and the scenario

Set the working method, the model and the project name, then write the scenario. Do not name the emotion ("sad"); write a visible action such as "hides the shard behind her back", which is much easier to split into cuts.

2) Register cast and background cards

This is the step that keeps the same person and the same place across cuts. On a set it corresponds to casting and locking the set. Register one card per person.

3) Set cut count and length, then read the written storyboard first

Set the count in Number of Panels and the length in Shot Length. Then press Text Preview Generate and a written storyboard for each cut appears before any image is made. If the order or the cast feels wrong, fixing at this stage is fastest.

4) Read the whole flow in Sheet view

Generating images gives you a grid of cuts. Sheet view gathers the cut images large, without the text. Looking at the pictures alone makes it easy to judge whether the cuts flow like real video and whether costume and props changed.

5) Fix only the cuts with problems

This is the heart of FLEXBOARD. With Edit on a cut card you can make a quick fix of an expression or prop, or a detailed fix of the camera (Shot Size, Camera Angle, Camera Movement). Write both what changes and what stays: for example, "change only the glasses, keep the pose and the background".

Two Claude skills work together

XCONDA has two skills for Claude.

1. FLEXBOARD skill
It cuts a scenario into shots. As it cuts, it checks that the shot lengths add up, that no shot crams in too much action, that cuts do not jump awkwardly, and that the number of characters is right. It returns a prompt to paste into FLEXBOARD together with a written storyboard listing length, camera, action, dialogue and sound effects for every cut.

2. XCONDA prompt skill
It builds a Seedance 2.5 prompt from the key images you pulled in FLEXBOARD. It organises the prompt with a four-director system and a ten-block structure, and lets you edit Korean and English side by side. Fix the Korean and the English is rewritten to match, so you never have to edit English separately.

The storyboard is what stops cuts from jumping

Video AI does not remember the previous cut. So when the cut changes, a bandage moves to the other arm, blood stains disappear and window frames change. Finding that after the video is finished is expensive; finding it at the storyboard stage costs one image.

Space in Turn, faces and framing in FLEXBOARD, and everything that must stay consistent pinned with references and prompts - that is written up as five steps in Nine cuts without the curse: how to cut it down with XCONDA today.

The same principle as an on-set storyboard

On drama sets it is common to shoot without a full read-through or a site visit, and to be handed last-minute pages. What sorts out the day's cuts quickly on location is the on-set storyboard. A storyboard cuts wasted trips, because fixing on paper before the camera rolls costs almost nothing, while fixing after the shoot means shooting again.

AI video is the same. Running video without a storyboard spends credits on wasted trips. FLEXBOARD is the on-set storyboard of AI video production.

Checklist before you hand over to Seedance

  • Did you check in Sheet view that the cuts connect naturally?
  • Do the image and the scene description match on every cut?
  • Are the cast tokens (@imageN) correct on every cut?
  • Is any cut missing?
  • Do shot length, frame size, camera angle and camera movement match the intent of the cut?

Common mistakes

  • Skipping the written storyboard and generating images. If the cut order is wrong, the images have to be made again.
  • Registering the same person on several cards. Tokens get confusing and faces get mixed.
  • Regenerating cuts that already work. Pick only the problem cuts and fix them with Edit.
  • Reviewing only in Panel view. Reading text makes it easy to miss whether the pictures connect. Read the pictures alone in Sheet view.
  • Switching image models halfway. The look shifts from cut to cut, so stay with the model you chose.

Frequently asked questions

Q. How many cuts, and how long, can FLEXBOARD make?
Up to 36 cuts on one screen, and each cut can be set between 4 and 30 seconds.

Q. How long a video can Seedance 2.5 make?
Per the developer ByteDance Seed, up to 30 seconds of video with sound in one run. Aspect ratio, resolution and duration are chosen in the settings bar of the XCONDA video generation screen, and prompts can be up to 30,000 characters.

Q. Where do I get the skills?
