The physics-inversion patent that fixes AI video instead of throwing it away

English · XCONDA Hub

Hello, this is XCONDA.

Cubeberry registered a patent (No. 10-3002930) for checking twice before rendering and telling the AI where, and by how much, something was wrong. This post covers the principle and the plan to bring it into XCONDA.

Who this is for: people who resent burning credits picking one usable take out of many, people worn down by hands passing through tables or objects floating in mid-air, and anyone curious about what Cubeberry has patented.

Generate ten, keep one or two

Anyone who has made AI video knows. Generate ten and one or two are usable; the rest look like this.

  • A hand passes through a table.
  • A cup floats in mid-air.
  • Someone running suddenly freezes.

So what do you do? Throw it away and run it again. Until it works.

This kept bothering CEO Jinseo Lee, because the cost of making the video is already spent by the time you throw it away. Work like that on a drama set and the producer yells at you.

On a drama set, checking comes first

Shooting a drama follows a fixed order. Check the storyboard, walk the blocking, and only then roll the camera. Checking first, shooting later. Over 20 years directing dramas as a PD, Jinseo Lee learned how much money that order saves.

This patent moves that order into AI.

Two checks before you render

Below is a plain-language explanation of the patent's concept. It is not a legal reading of the claims.

First check: does it break the settings already decided?
We see whether the scene fits the settings established in advance. An introverted character suddenly turning violent, or a car appearing in a medieval setting, is caught here.

Second check: does the movement make physical sense?
If it passes the first, we stand the people and objects up as simple shapes and turn them gently — a rehearsal before the shoot. Here we catch objects passing through each other, defying gravity, or movement that stops unnaturally. Because this happens before the real video, it costs almost nothing.

When a problem is caught: tell, do not throw away
When a problem is found, we do not just discard it. We extract where and how badly it went wrong and turn that into a form the AI can understand, then feed it back. The goal is for the same mistake not to happen in the same place next time.

Frequently asked

Q. Can I use this feature now?
Not yet. It is at the planning stage and no release date is set.

Q. How do I waste fewer videos today?
Before rendering, confirm the cuts as images in FLEXBOARD. A wrong cut costs one image to remake, which uses far fewer credits than re-running the whole video. The detailed method is in the post below.

Q. How is this different from the problem of cuts jumping?
The continuity problem is about props or costumes changing between cuts; this patent looks at whether movement within a single scene makes physical sense. They are different problems, which is why there are two patents.
